## Objective
Resolve and record the product/domain semantics for the first Pricing & Promotion slice before any Pricing application code is written.

This task is a product/domain design gate. It must leave a deterministic policy that a Technical Architect and Builder can implement without inventing promotion behavior.

## Context
CommerceOS already reserves Pricing & Promotion as the owner of future authoritative offer/discount rules. Catalog owns canonical Product and base selling price; Sales owns the immutable accepted Order snapshot. `PD-013` explicitly excludes manual authoritative guest-checkout discounts from the MVP and requires future discount behavior to be introduced through an explicit Pricing product decision.

The previous version of this task mixed product decisions, technical design and implementation in one XL task. That made it possible for a Builder to guess business semantics while coding. This task now contains design only; technical design and implementation are split into TASK-0242 and TASK-0243.

## Human-approved starting constraints — 2026-08-14

The Product Owner approved the following direction for the first slice:

1. The first capability is a **scheduled Product promotional price**.
2. A promotion defines an explicit authoritative **final promotional unit price** rather than a percentage discount or fixed discount amount.
3. One promotion targets one Product and applies to all shoppers using the public storefront checkout path.
4. The first slice has no coupons, manual merchant-entered order discounts, Category/cart/order-wide promotions, customer segments, price lists, Buy-X-Get-Y rules or other promotion-rule engine behavior.
5. Promotions do not stack. Overlapping effective promotion periods for the same Product must not be accepted in the first slice.
6. Owner and Admin may manage promotions. Staff and Viewer may not create, change, schedule or cancel them.
7. Catalog remains authority for Product base price and sell/public eligibility. Pricing may change the effective commercial price but may never make an unpublished/archived/ineligible Product sellable.
8. Pricing is authoritative for the effective promotional price. Browser/cart-provided discount or total values are never authoritative.
9. Sales preserves immutable accepted pricing provenance. Later changes to Product base price or Promotion state never reprice an existing Order.
10. Accounting and refund behavior for this first slice uses the **effective price actually accepted on the Order** as the commercial amount. Do not introduce a contra-revenue/discount account merely for this slice.
11. Checkout keeps the existing authoritative repricing/reconfirmation rule: if the current effective price differs from the shopper's previously observed price, the shopper must reconfirm and no Order is created for that attempt.

These constraints narrow the design but do not by themselves close the remaining lifecycle/time/history questions below.

## Scope
Resolve and document the remaining product/domain semantics for the approved first slice:

- Promotion identity and lifecycle.
- Creation, scheduling, activation, cancellation and terminal/history behavior.
- Whether scheduled or already-active promotions may be edited, and which fields become immutable after activation.
- Effective-period semantics, including Tenant business timezone input, canonical stored/evaluated instant, and exact start/end boundary rules.
- Interaction with later Catalog base-price edits while a promotion is scheduled or active.
- Validation when promotional price is zero, greater than/equal to base price, or becomes unattractive after a later base-price change.
- Public storefront presentation: effective price only versus base-price comparison, and whether schedule/end information is public.
- Exact immutable pricing provenance Sales must retain conceptually: base price, effective price, Promotion identity/version/decision reference and evaluation time as appropriate.
- Non-destructive retention/history rules needed to explain an accepted historical Order.
- Explicit confirmation that Pricing affects price only and never overrides Catalog publication/sellability authority.

Record the approved decision and rationale in `docs/domains/product-decisions.md` as a new Pricing/Promotion decision (next available PD id), then reconcile the relevant domain baseline and requirement/planning references.

## Out of scope
- Pricing application/domain/infrastructure code.
- Persistence schema, API/DTO shape, LocalStack resource selection or transport topology.
- Coupons, percentage/fixed discounts, manual checkout discounts, promotion stacking/priority engine, customer-specific pricing, Category/cart/order promotions, price lists, variants or flash-sale orchestration.
- Repricing historical Orders.
- Changing Catalog/Sales/Accounting ownership boundaries to simplify implementation.

2. Enumerate the remaining lifecycle/time/history alternatives and their business consequences.
3. Obtain or apply explicit Product Owner decisions; do not infer them from storage/API convenience.

5. Reconcile Pricing, Catalog, Storefront/Checkout, Sales, Accounting and Refund domain consequences without selecting technical mechanisms.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] A new explicit Pricing/Promotion product decision records the approved first-slice semantics and rationale.
- [ ] Promotion lifecycle, edit/cancel/history behavior and time-boundary semantics are deterministic.
- [ ] Base-price-change interaction is deterministic for scheduled and active promotions.
- [ ] Validation rules for the promotional unit price are explicit.

- [ ] Owner/Admin mutation authority and Staff/Viewer denial are explicit.
- [ ] Pricing owns effective promotional price; Catalog retains base price and sellability; Sales retains immutable accepted Order pricing provenance.
- [ ] Client discount/total is never authoritative and the existing checkout repricing/reconfirmation invariant remains intact.
- [ ] Historical Orders are never repriced by later Catalog/Promotion changes.
- [ ] Accounting/refund semantics use the accepted effective Order amount for this slice and do not require a new discount accounting model.
- [ ] Coupons/stacking/rule-engine/customer-specific/manual-discount semantics remain explicitly out of scope.

## Architecture / security / reliability / observability / local runtime
This task chooses no infrastructure mechanism. Tenant ownership and Merchant Access authority are product/domain constraints only. TASK-0242 will resolve contracts, persistence, concurrency enforcement, observability and LocalStack topology after this task is complete.

## Risks and edge cases
- Treating a scheduled final price as a generic discount engine would silently expand scope.
- Allowing active promotion edits without explicit history/version semantics can make historical Order explanations ambiguous.
- Catalog base-price changes during an active promotion can produce counterintuitive pricing unless the policy is explicit.
- Timezone and interval-boundary ambiguity can create overlapping or double-active promotions.

## Open questions
The remaining design questions are intentionally limited to lifecycle/editability, time boundaries, base-price interaction, promotional-price validation, public presentation and historical retention/provenance. They must be closed here before TASK-0242 becomes Ready.
